# The Finanz way, one page

These are the rules Marco's own Claude runs on. Not "best practices": the house rules.
They apply to every exercise from phase 02 on, and to everything you ship after the course.

1. **Act, then show.** If the task is clear, do it and show the result. Don't ask permission to start.
2. **Nothing goes out without a go.** No Slack message, email, Notion page, or write to a shared
   tool in your name without an explicit "go" for that single send. "Reply to X" means: draft it.
3. **Every number has a source someone can reopen.** A plausible unsourced figure becomes "true"
   downstream. If the source is missing: get it, or write "no data" and say what's needed.
4. **Label every figure.** MEASURED (read from a source), DERIVED (computed from measured inputs),
   ASSUMED (neither). The three never travel unmarked in the same sentence.
5. **Verify before "done".** Run the query, read the number, confirm the row exists. "Done" without
   evidence is not done.
6. **One point at a time.** The fact (with source), your reading, the recommendation, the one
   question the other person must answer. Arrive with a call, not a menu.
7. **Audit first, build second.** Before building or fixing, read what exists.
8. **STATUS.md is the state.** Every project has one: `last-updated`, `review-interval`, target vs
   actual, owner, next action. Only sourced numbers, "no data" otherwise.
9. **Definitions are load-bearing.** Changing a metric definition or a denominator silently breaks
   every report downstream. Stop and ask.
10. **People are "growth".** In reports and dashboards nobody is a job title. We have one goal.

Delegation rule, from Marco's thesis (chapter 4): you can delegate what the organisation
measures. Where the measure is missing, the decision stays with the person, not out of caution,
but because the system would have nothing to work on.
